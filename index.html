Types I–III are antibody-mediated; type IV is T cell-mediated. Types II and III share antibodies and effector tools and differ only in whether the antigen is fixed or soluble.

Immunologic tolerance and autoimmunity

Central vs peripheral tolerance Tolerance

T cellsB cells
Central
Primary lymphoid organ, immature cell
Thymus. Negative selection: thymocytes binding self peptide–MHC with high affinity are deleted. AIRE drives expression of tissue-restricted antigens in medullary thymic epithelial cells so they can be tested against. Loss → APECEDBone marrow. High avidity (multivalent, membrane-bound antigen) → receptor editing (light chain V–J recombination resumes, new specificity) or deletion by apoptosis. Low avidity (soluble, monomeric) → anergy with reduced surface IgM
Peripheral
Mature cell, secondary tissues
Anergy (antigen without costimulation), Treg suppression, deletion, inhibitory receptors (CTLA-4 early at priming, PD-1 later in tissue), ignoranceAnergy (repeated self-antigen binding without T cell help; shortened lifespan), deletion, inhibitory receptors (e.g., FcγRIIB)

Central tolerance is necessarily incomplete — not every self antigen is represented, some appear later in life, and the deletion threshold deliberately spares weakly self-reactive cells — so peripheral mechanisms are always required.

Why tolerance breaks Tolerance

FactorDetail
Genetic: HLALargest single contribution (~50% of genetic susceptibility). Necessary but not sufficient — risk haplotypes are common in healthy people and monozygotic concordance is well below 100%
Genetic: non-HLAPTPN22, CTLA4, CD25 (IL2RA), FCGRIIB, IL23R, NOD2 — genes of immune response and regulation. Small individual effects, shared across diseases, so one patient may have two or more
EnvironmentalInfection, chronic inflammation, hormonal status, pollutants and carcinogens
Molecular mimicryA microbial peptide resembles a self peptide → cross-activation of autoreactive T or B cells. The target tissue is set by the self antigen, not by where the microbe was, and the response persists after clearance. Rheumatic fever (cardiac myosin / S. pyogenes), MS (myelin basic protein / EBV), Graves' (TSH receptor / Yersinia), SLE (spliceosome / EBV)
Bystander activationInfection activates tissue APCs → B7 and cytokines up → self-reactive T cells finally get signal 2. Disease appears in the infected tissue, with no sequence similarity required
Tissue injuryUses the same effector mechanisms as hypersensitivity types II, III and IV; organ-specific vs systemic follows the distribution of the target antigen
